# Large-Domain, Nonlinear System Identification of UAVs
The motivating application of wind inference from aircraft motion requires flight dynamic models that are valid across a wide range of flight conditions. When used for control and estimation, multirotor motion models are almost always linear, small-perturbation models; nonlinear flight dynamic models that accurately predict motion over a large operating envelope are rare, even though there is a critical need for large-envelope control. I developed a \textbf{widely-applicable nonlinear multirotor flight dynamic model based on blade-element and momentum theory of rotorcraft that is identifiable from flight data}~\cite{hopwoodGenericNonlinearFlightInReview,hopwoodDevelopmentEvaluationMultirotor2024}.

Identifying this large-domain nonlinear model is especially challenging due to the unstable nature of multirotor flight. In order to obtain the rich, large-envelope input/output data necessary for nonlinear model identification, one must first stabilize flight through feedback, typically using a rudimentary, model-free control method, since no model is yet available for model-based control design. But there is no guarantee such a rudimentary controller will be robust to the input excitations required for model identification. Furthermore, stabilizing control laws often introduce correlation, making accurate parameter estimation difficult. For these reasons, I developed a \textbf{framework for obtaining flight data that leverages stability guarantees from a robust linear parameter-varying $H_2$/$H_\infty$ control law that executes a reference signal specially designed to decorrelate model regressors}~\cite{hopwoodRobustLinearParameterVarying2024,hopwoodRobustLPVInPneparation}. This work has contributed an important new method to obtain nonlinear models for unstable systems that are valid across a wide range of operating conditions.
