# Wind Estimation for Aircraft Maneuvering in Stochastic Environments
The culmination of my doctoral research is the application of novel nonlinear, model-based observer theory to wind estimation for aircraft maneuvering in turbulence, such as UAVs monitoring wildfires or air mobility aircraft operating near vertiports. Typical approaches to wind estimation lose their guarantees during aggressive maneuvering and in severe turbulence. The benefit of using nonlinear observers is that the convergence guarantees of the wind estimate to its true value still hold for general maneuvering, as might happen in a disaster response scenario within an urban canopy. The incorporation of stochastic dynamics and the resulting stochastic stability guarantees on the state estimate error system extends these guarantees even to turbulent conditions where safety assurance is of great importance.

Nearly all model-based wind estimation approaches estimate not only the unknown wind and air-relative velocity, but also directly measured values like position, attitude, and angular velocity. The symmetry-preserving reduced-order observer I developed estimates only wind and air-relative velocity, reducing computational complexity and simplifying nonlinear stability analysis. Ongoing work involves \textbf{flight testing of this symmetry-preserving reduced-order wind observer for general maneuvering flight}~\cite{hopwoodSymmetryPreservingFormulationPassivityBasedToAppear} using the research UAS shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:mtd}, which is equipped with an attritable air data unit I developed for wind estimate validation. By exploiting the invariance of aircraft dynamics under rotations and translations, the nonlinear wind estimation problem simplifies to the design of a linear, time-invariant observer. The dynamical symmetry allows for a straightforward extension to handle turbulence, enabling \textbf{explicit solutions for the stochastic state estimation error dynamics and ensuring exponential convergence of the error's probability density to a stationary distribution}~\cite{hopwoodNoisetoStateStableReducedOrderToAppear}. These advances extend the operational envelope for both aerial robotics and passenger aircraft, with broad safety implications for many industries such as ground and underwater vehicles.

For the deterministic case, another powerful tool in observer design is feedback passivity, where one aims to craft the observer output injection to make the state estimation error dynamics passive. I have developed a \textbf{nonlinear passivity-based observer for wind estimation}, designed to provide global exponential convergence guarantees for maneuvering flight in a steady, uniform wind field~\cite{hopwoodPassivityBasedWindEstimation2024}. This approach provides a linear matrix inequality (LMI)-based design, which can be used to optimize the wind estimate convergence rate. Implementing this observer on flight test data (Fig.~\ref{fig:pbo}) with the research UAS shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:mtd} has demonstrated its ability to estimate wind with high accuracy during maneuvering flight, even in turbulent air. 

The validity of wind estimates relies heavily on the aerodynamic model used in model-based estimator design. An enabling approach to characterize~\cite{gahanWindEstimateUncertaintyInReview,gahanUncertaintyWindEstimates2024} (and even reduce~\cite{gahanUncertaintyWindEstimates2024a}) the effect of parametric uncertainty on wind estimates is to use \textbf{generalized polynomial chaos (gPC)} to expand the uncertain dynamics into a higher-dimensional deterministic system for which standard analysis and estimation may be performed. In collaborative work, I used an $H_\infty$ filtering approach in which the \textbf{bulk flow component of the wind is estimated with worst case bounds on the effect of turbulence}~\cite{gahanModelBasedWind2024}.

Another way to increase trust in model-based wind estimates is by incorporating flight dynamic models that capture unsteady aerodynamics. In other collaborative work, I helped to identify an \textbf{unsteady aerodynamic model from flight data which was integrated into an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for wind estimation}~\cite{halefomUnsteadyAerodynamicsModelBased2024}. The results showed comparable performance to a quasi-steady model, but demonstrated the inclusion of unsteady effects improves the reconstruction of turbulence spectra. This improvement is especially relevant for atmospheric science, where characterization of turbulence is important for weather modeling and forecasting.
